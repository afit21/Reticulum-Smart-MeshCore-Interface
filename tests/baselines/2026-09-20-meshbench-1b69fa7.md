## How these were produced (reproduce the same way)

    python3 testscripts/meshbench_scenarios.py run <scenario> --capture-dir /tmp/mb/<label>/<scenario>-<n>

MeshBench v0.1.0, native MeshCore v1.17.1 firmware (companion_radio / simple_repeater), radios
916.575 MHz / 62.5 kHz / SF7 / CR8, seed 7, production timing, the script's default probe counts
(zero_hop and relay 8 x 32 B, two_hop 8 x 32 B, large_payload 6 x 383 B = 4 raw fragments), two
runs at a time on the desktop, each run against a snapshot of the interface pinned with
`SMCI_INTERFACE_PATH` (so an edit to the working tree cannot leak into a running scenario).
Each row was derived from the run's `run.log`, the two interface captures (`capture_A_*.jsonl`,
`capture_B_*.jsonl`) and `meshbench_events.jsonl` with the session's summariser; the columns are:

- verdict and probes delivered (delivered = the PROOF came back within the 60 s probe timeout);
- probe RTT min/avg/max over the delivered probes;
- RNS path time on the sender (harness `path_resolved` - `iface_online`) / seconds from each
  interface's `online` log line to its first `path discovered to` line;
- DIRECT attempts ok/total by `hop_count` with the median `ack_latency_s` (from `direct_attempt_result`);
- the sender's `completion_check_result` records answered-or-reported / total (rep = `outcome="reported"`);
- raw fragments the sender put on air (`raw_fragment_sent`, by `round`);
- MeshBench transmissions / bytes / seconds on air per node (each `tx` line's detail);
- top miss reasons from `meshbench_events.jsonl` (half-duplex = the receiver's own transmitter was
  keyed; locked = demodulator locked to another frame; collision = header decoded then lost; snr).

## Read these numbers with two things in mind

1. MeshBench's RF is deliberately optimistic, its airtime 1.2-1.45x RadioLib's, and its runs are
   wall-clock driven and NOT reproducible: the same tree gave `zero_hop` 7/8 PASS and 6/8 FAIL,
   `large_payload` 1/6 FAIL and 4/6 PASS, and `two_hop` anything from "no DIRECT path in 7 minutes"
   to 5/8. A difference inside that spread is not evidence. Mechanics (topology gate, hop count,
   repeaters relayed, staged events) are the hard checks; delivery is a measured rate.
2. MeshBench's virtual radio has NO listen-before-talk (verified 2026-09-20 against the relay-1
   event log -- 23 of 52 half-duplex misses were a node keying 20-980 ms into a frame it was already
   receiving -- and against the firmware's `Dispatcher::checkSend`, which defers while `isReceiving()`).
   It therefore over-counts self-collisions between nodes that can hear each other (zero hop; a
   sender against its own repeater at one hop, where about half of every raw burst is lost here but
   ~18% per fragment in the field), while hidden-node collisions at a repeater are real. Field
   figures for comparison (2026-09-19): hop 0 ~1.3 s ACK / 92-96% attempt success; hop 1 ~3.1 s / 65%;
   hop 2 ~4.0 s / 51%; hop 3 ~5.6 s / 42%.

## What to compare a future change against

- **Zero hop** (report mechanism, DIRECT fast path): delivered 7-8/8; probe RTT avg 5-8 s; DIRECT
  attempt success ~100%; 2-16 half-duplex misses per run; endpoint transmissions ~45+28 to 47+38;
  `completion_check_result` all `reported`, 0-2 `completion_query` attempts.
- **Relay, one hop**: delivered 7-8/8; probe RTT avg 8.6-13.6 s; R relays 63-75 frames;
  endpoint transmissions ~40-48 each; 7-8 of 9-12 completion checks reported.
- **large_payload, one hop, 4-fragment raw parts**: delivered 1-6/6 (that wide); delivered-probe
  RTT avg 29.6-41.9 s; first bursts land 1-3 of 4 fragments (MeshBench's LBT artefact -- not the
  field); per-part first-fragment-to-known-complete 14.5-44.9 s; 6-14 reports vs 5-11 QUERY
  attempts per run; R relays 100-141 frames; A 82-105 transmissions.
- **two_hop**: bring-up dominated (DIRECT path at 138-543 s or never); delivered 2-5/8; zero
  `unknown_dest_backoff_drop`. A change to discovery/binding is what would move this scenario.
- **Timing mechanics** (from `direct_attempt_result` / `completion_check_result`): missed-ACK
  `ack_timeout_s` <= 8 s at one hop, <= 11 s at two; post-miss `listen_delay_s` 0.2-1.0 s;
  completion checks after an un-ACKed QUERY end within 6 s (10 s from two hops).

A mechanics FAIL (topology, hop count, repeater relayed, staged event) is a regression; a delivery
or timing difference inside the spreads above is not evidence either way -- run a third time.
